Bangladesh Power Disruption Triggers Internet Traffic Decline of More Than 27%

Event Date: October 27, 2026  |  Location: Bangladesh  |  Category: ISP / Network Infrastructure Incident

A power disruption reported in Bangladesh at approximately 10:00 AM Bangladesh Standard Time (BST, UTC+6) on October 17, 2026, coincided with an internet traffic decline of more than 27% during the day, according to network monitoring observations.

The event highlights the operational dependency between electricity availability and telecommunications infrastructure, including access networks, mobile base stations, aggregation equipment, and ISP points of presence.

The exact geographical extent of the power disruption, the affected operators, the duration of the traffic decline, and the underlying electrical fault have not been independently confirmed.

Incident Overview

On October 17, 2026, network monitoring observations indicated a significant reduction in internet traffic associated with a reported power disruption in Bangladesh. The disruption began at approximately 12:00 Beijing Time, equivalent to 04:00 UTC.

The observed traffic decline exceeded 27% during the day. Such a change warrants investigation into the availability of telecommunications infrastructure, the operating status of local service providers, and the resilience of the affected networks during a power-related incident.

Electricity interruptions can affect telecommunications equipment through the loss of commercial power, depleted backup batteries, generator failures, or interruptions to supporting facilities. However, these are potential mechanisms rather than confirmed causes of this incident.

Network Impact

Power-related incidents can affect internet services at multiple layers of the telecommunications network. The actual impact depends on the location of the electrical failure, the resilience of local facilities, and the availability of backup power.

1. Access Network Availability

Broadband access equipment, mobile base stations, optical access nodes, and local aggregation devices depend on reliable electricity. If commercial power is interrupted and backup systems cannot maintain operations, subscribers may experience connectivity loss, reduced coverage, or intermittent service.

2. Mobile Network Services

Mobile sites typically use backup batteries and may have generator support at selected locations. An outage can therefore produce different results across sites: some remain operational, while others may become unavailable after their backup power is exhausted.

Site-level power alarms, battery runtime, generator status, and radio-access availability can help operators distinguish an electrical incident from a transport or core-network failure.

3. ISP Points of Presence and Aggregation

Power interruptions at ISP facilities, aggregation locations, or upstream connectivity sites may affect larger traffic volumes than isolated access-site failures. The scale of the impact depends on network topology, equipment redundancy, and the distribution of affected facilities.

4. International Connectivity and CDN Traffic

A reduction in traffic originating from Bangladesh may also be visible on international transit links, peering sessions, and CDN delivery platforms.

CDN operators should determine whether the decline occurred across multiple access networks or was concentrated in a particular ISP, ASN, region, or cache group. A country-level traffic decline alone cannot identify which infrastructure component failed.

Routing Analysis

A power-related traffic decline does not necessarily produce an immediate change in the global BGP routing table. The relationship depends on where the electrical failure occurs and whether the affected equipment supports routing, transport, or subscriber access.

  • Stable BGP with declining traffic: If prefix announcements and peer sessions remain stable while traffic falls, the investigation should include access-network availability, local power systems, transport capacity, and changes in demand.
  • BGP withdrawals: If prefixes disappear during the incident, identify the affected ASNs and prefixes, then correlate the changes with power alarms, device logs, and upstream reachability.
  • Peering and transit changes: Check whether BGP sessions reset, interface availability changed, or traffic moved to alternative upstream paths.
  • Traffic redistribution: If traffic decreases on one provider but increases on another, investigate route changes, CDN steering, and shifts between access networks before attributing the entire change to service loss.

No event-specific BGP telemetry has been supplied for October 27. Accordingly, this report does not conclude that Bangladesh experienced a nationwide routing failure, a BGP withdrawal event, or a loss of international connectivity.

Possible Cause

The reported power disruption is a potential explanation for the observed traffic decline. Additional evidence is required to establish the relationship between the electrical incident and the network impact.

Commercial Power Interruption

A loss of commercial power at telecommunications sites can cause equipment to operate on backup batteries or generators. If backup systems fail or their operating duration is exceeded, individual sites may become unavailable.

Backup Power Limitations

Battery degradation, insufficient backup capacity, generator startup failures, and fuel availability can affect the resilience of telecommunications facilities during an extended electrical interruption.

Transport and Aggregation Dependencies

Even when individual access sites remain operational, the loss of power at an aggregation facility, transmission node, or upstream location can interrupt connectivity for multiple downstream sites.

Other Potential Contributors

Changes in network routing, upstream connectivity, traffic demand, or service availability may contribute to the measured decline. These possibilities should be investigated independently rather than assumed to be consequences of the power disruption.

Operational Impact for ISPs and CDN Operators

For network operators, the main challenge is determining whether the observed traffic reduction reflects a loss of subscriber connectivity, an upstream transport issue, or a redistribution of traffic across networks.

  • Traffic monitoring: Compare current traffic against the same period on previous days and against the normal hourly baseline.
  • ASN-level analysis: Identify which origin networks contribute most to the decline and whether the change is isolated to a small number of providers.
  • Regional correlation: Compare traffic changes across cities, access networks, and points of presence.
  • CDN cache analysis: Check request volume, delivered bandwidth, cache hit ratio, origin fetches, and error rates to determine whether the traffic change is accompanied by degraded service.
  • Routing verification: Correlate BGP updates, peer status, interface counters, and path changes with the traffic decline.
  • Recovery validation: Confirm that traffic, request volume, subscriber availability, and network alarms return to their normal operating ranges.

Recommended Operator Checks

Power and Facility Monitoring

  • Review commercial power alarms and facility power events around 04:00 UTC on October 17.
  • Check battery runtime, rectifier status, generator alarms, and equipment restart records.
  • Correlate power events with the availability of access, aggregation, and transmission equipment.

Network Availability

  • Compare ICMP reachability, TCP connection success, packet loss, and latency before, during, and after the event.
  • Review BGP session changes, route withdrawals, prefix visibility, and upstream reachability.
  • Check interface errors, link transitions, and transport alarms on affected network devices.

Traffic and Service Quality

  • Compare the traffic decline across ASNs, individual providers, regions, and international transit links.
  • Check CDN request counts, bandwidth, HTTP error rates, cache hit ratios, and origin connectivity.
  • Verify whether traffic recovery follows the restoration of power and network availability.

These checks can help establish the event's scope and identify its most likely failure domain. They are recommendations for investigation, not evidence that any specific failure occurred during this event.
