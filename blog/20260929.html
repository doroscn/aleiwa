Myanmar TikTok Access Disruptions Observed on September 28–29, 2026

2026-09-29 — ISP / Access Restriction

Network monitoring observed significant declines in TikTok video traffic originating from Myanmar during two separate periods on September 28 and September 29, 2026. Internal measurements indicate that traffic fell by approximately 50% during the affected periods, with the impact concentrated on TikTok video access and delivery.

What We Observed

  • September 28, 13:55 MMT: A sharp decline in TikTok video traffic from Myanmar was detected.
  • September 29, 15:25 MMT: A second significant decline in TikTok video traffic from Myanmar was detected.
  • Internal monitoring showed an overall traffic reduction of approximately 50% compared with the normal baseline during the affected periods.
  • The impact was primarily visible in TikTok video traffic rather than representing a complete loss of Internet connectivity from Myanmar.

Network Impact

  • The observed impact was geographically concentrated on traffic originating from Myanmar.
  • The reduction was particularly visible in video delivery traffic, which can directly affect CDN and cache traffic even when general Internet connectivity remains available.
  • The observed pattern is different from a global TikTok platform outage because the traffic change was concentrated on the Myanmar market.

External Signals

Public reports on September 28 indicated that some users in Myanmar were experiencing problems accessing TikTok or loading video content, with reports that access could work through a VPN. Independent service-monitoring data also recorded TikTok connectivity reports from Myanmar, including Yangon, during the same period.

These external reports provide supporting evidence that the observed traffic reduction was not limited to a single local network measurement. However, public sources do not independently confirm the exact mechanism responsible for the traffic changes observed by Aleiwa.

Possible Cause

The timing and service-specific nature of the traffic reduction are consistent with localized access restrictions affecting TikTok. Myanmar has an ongoing history of Internet and digital-platform restrictions in the context of its broader political and security situation.

At present, the available evidence does not establish which organization or network mechanism caused the observed disruption. Possible technical mechanisms could include DNS interference, destination filtering, TCP connection failure, traffic inspection, or other forms of network-level access control.

Timeline

  • 2026-09-28 13:55 MMT — TikTok video traffic from Myanmar begins a significant decline.
  • 2026-09-29 15:25 MMT — A second significant decline is observed in internal monitoring.
  • 2026-09-29 — Aleiwa records the event as a localized TikTok access disruption affecting Myanmar traffic.

Aleiwa Analysis

The available measurements indicate a localized access disruption affecting TikTok video traffic from Myanmar, with an estimated traffic reduction of approximately 50% during the observed periods. The event does not currently appear to represent a general Internet outage across Myanmar.

For network operators, further validation should focus on DNS resolution, TCP connectivity to TikTok delivery endpoints, HTTP or HTTPS reachability, and comparisons across different Myanmar access networks. Correlating connection failures with CDN traffic and cache metrics can help distinguish access restrictions from ordinary traffic fluctuations or service-side problems.

Sources

  • Public user reports — Myanmar users reporting TikTok access and video-loading problems on September 28, 2026
  • Independent service monitoring — TikTok service reports including activity associated with Myanmar/Yangon
  • Myanmar digital restrictions reporting — Reporting on continuing digital restrictions in Myanmar during 2026
